# Value flow

**Audience:** reader wanting to understand where real value enters, transits, and exits the protocol.
**Prerequisites:** [Burn-to-mint](../02-core-concepts/03-burn-to-mint.md), [Treasury and fees](../02-core-concepts/07-treasury-and-fees.md).

## Where real value comes from

The only source of **external value** in the system is the end user paying for CREDIT. They pay because they need to use the apps. If nobody wants to use the apps, nobody buys CREDIT, and the system dies — that is the hard invariant.

The typical path:

```
   [ User Charlie ]
        |
        |  1. Buys 1000 CREDIT on external DEX for $X in USDC
        |     (liquidity provided by whoever has CREDIT and wants to exit,
        |      or by off-protocol liquidity mining — not inside here)
        |
        v
   [ Charlie's wallet has 1000 CREDIT ]
        |
        |  2. Uses the Chat App. App charges 1000 CREDIT for the service.
        |     Charlie signs approve(feeRouter, 1000) + feeRouter.pay(...)
        |
        v
   [ FeeRouter ]
        |
        | split 95/0/5 default
        |
        +-------> 950 CREDIT burned (via BurnTracker)
        |         - CREDIT.totalSupply decreases
        |         - burn recorded in BurnTracker for Chat App
        |
        +-------> 50 CREDIT -> app owner (direct rebate)
```

**Real value entered the protocol at step 1.** All subsequent steps redistribute that value. None of them creates value out of thin air.

Notice: **there is no "thin air" yield**. The CREDIT emitted to Alice exists because Charlie burned CREDIT. The rebate to ChatApp exists because Charlie paid. No actor receives value that did not come from some upstream actor.

## The 3 revenue sources of an app

The direct 5% default split seems small. But the numbers work out — if the app also stakes in its own project — via three simultaneous vectors:

### (A) Direct rebate

5% of every payment. Instantaneous, in CREDIT. It is the operational cash flow.

### (B) Emission via stake in own `projectId`

If the app acquired GOV (e.g., by buying on DEX or receiving via a distribution proposal) and staked in `projectId = own`, it captures part of the reward share of that project in the following round.

How?

1. When Charlie pays, 95% is burned and goes to `burnByRoundProject[R][projectId=app]`.
2. In round R+1, emission is proportional to that burn.
3. If the app has staking weight inside its own `projectId`, it receives part of that emission.

**Numeric example** (hypothetical round):

- ChatApp moves 600,000 CREDIT in payments in round R-1.
- (A) Direct rebate: 5% × 600k = **30,000 CREDIT** immediate.
- In round R, `projectShare_ChatApp = 902,500 × 600k/950k = 570,000 CREDIT`.
- If ChatApp staked 50k GOV with a 365d lock (multiplier 4x, weight 200k), and total weight in the project is 400k, it captures 50% × 570k = **285,000 CREDIT**.
- Sum (A) + (B) = **315,000 CREDIT** out of a gross volume of 600k = **~52.5%** effective.

The **nominal** split tells a misleading story. The **effective economic split** for an app that also stakes depends on how much it stakes and the competition of other stakers in the same `projectId`.

### (C) Retained CREDIT appreciation

Since `alpha < 1`, CREDIT supply drops if usage stays constant. The CREDIT the app received (rebate + rewards) and has not yet sold tends to appreciate in real terms — as long as CREDIT demand (generated by app usage) sustains.

**Trap**: if the app sells all CREDIT immediately, it gives up (C). If it retains, it is exposed to volatility. That is the app's call.

## When (B) does not close the gap

Apps without capital to acquire GOV do not capture (B). For those cases:

- The default split (95/0/5) can be **disincentivizing**.
- The DAO can approve `setProjectSplit(projectId, custom)` via proposal. For example: `8000/0/2000` (20% rebate) for strategic apps that cannot stake.
- Alternatively, the Treasury can fund initial GOV acquisition (direct transfer proposal).

The architecture allows per-project adjustment precisely for that type of accommodation.

   |   burnByRoundProject[R-1][42] = 600_000                |                                    |

                                                                     |

                                                                     |

                                                                max(0.95 * 950_000, floor(R-1)),

## CREDIT rotation

CREDIT enters supply through only **two** paths:

1. `CreditToken.mintGenesis` — one-shot, 10M to Treasury, at deploy.
2. `CreditToken.mint` — by `RewardDistributor`, on claims.

And exits through **one** path:

- `CreditToken.burn` / `burnFrom` / `burnByRole` — main path is via `FeeRouter.pay` → `BurnTracker.burnAndRecord` → `CreditToken.burnByRole`.

This closed flow allows a simple analysis:

```
   totalSupply_R = totalSupply_{R-1} + (new emissions in R) - (burns in R)
```

If `new emissions <= burns`, supply drops. With `alpha = 0.95` and stable usage, that is the expected dynamic.

## GOV rotation

GOV enters supply permanently until it reaches the cap:

1. Genesis: 0 at deploy.
2. `mint`: only by the owner (Timelock in production) until reaching `CAP_SUPPLY = 100M`.
3. After reaching the cap, `mint` reverts with `CapExceeded`.

There is no GOV burn in the code.

Circulation:

- **Free holder** → DEX buy/sell.
- **Holder → Staking**: `stake` locks GOV in the contract; `unstake` releases it.
- **Holder → Registry**: `registerProject` locks GOV as collateral; `removeProject` releases it (to owner or treasury).
- **Holder → TeamVesting (via Timelock)**: vesting contracts that release gradually.
- **Holder → Governor**: `delegate` does not move GOV, only confers voting power.

## The role of the Treasury in this flow

The Treasury is the system's **shock absorber**. It:

- Receives the CREDIT genesis (10M).
- Can receive `treasuryBps` of payments (0% in default).
- Receives collateral from projects removed with slash.
- Can be funded by GOV buyback (via `executeBuyback`, stubbed in v1).

And distributes, via proposals:

- Subsidies for new users (`UserSubsidy`).
- Vesting for the team (`TeamVesting`).
- Batch rebate payments for apps.
- Funding for off-chain operations.

The Treasury is **not** distributed automatically. Everything flows out via proposal.

## Economic health invariant

A simple health signal for the protocol: **cumulative burn must grow faster than cumulative emission**, over rounds. Since `alpha < 1` guarantees `emission_R ≈ 0.95 × burn_{R-1}`, that inequality is automatically satisfied as long as:

```
burn_R >= burn_{R-1}
```

That is: the protocol is healthy while real usage holds or grows. If burn drops, emission drops with it, but the `emission/burn` ratio remains constant at `alpha`. The terminal symptom is absolute burn going to zero for many consecutive rounds.
